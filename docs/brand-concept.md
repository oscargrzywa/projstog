
# PROJSTOG — Brand Concept Spec

## The One-Liner
PROJSTOG builds done-for-you websites for Polish local service businesses — fixed scope, live in 14 days, made to bring in work instead of just sitting there.

## The Product
PROJSTOG sells the **Profit Site**: a five-page, mobile-first website built for one local service business, handed over finished and already earning.

It is not a "platform," a template you assemble yourself, or hours billed against a vague scope. It is a fixed deliverable. You buy a complete website — Home, Offer, Work (Realizacje), About, and Contact with a built-in brief form — written, designed, and engineered by PROJSTOG, then connected to your Google Business Profile so you show up when someone in your town searches for what you do.

What it does: it turns the people already looking you up — on Google, on Instagram, off a van decal — into quote requests and bookings. Every page pushes one action. The site loads fast on a phone, reads premium, and answers "how good / how do I book" before the customer has to send a DM.

How it's delivered: a 14-day build on a fixed timeline. Day 1 brief, day 3 blueprint sign-off, day 10 review, day 14 live. After launch it runs on a **Live Care** plan so the owner never touches code, hosting, or updates again. The product is the finished, maintained site — not the software it's made of.

## The Customer
The customer is the Polish local service business owner — the kind of business PROJSTOG already builds for: detailing studios, steel fabricators, real-estate agents, trades, and one- to ten-person service firms in towns and mid-size cities across the country.

They do genuinely good work. The problem is that nobody can tell that before they show up. The business runs off an Instagram account and a phone that never stops buzzing with "ile kosztuje?" messages answered late at night. There's no real website, or there's a dead one. The premium customers — the higher-ticket jobs — go to a competitor who does worse work but has a clean site and a clear way to get in touch.

What they want: more of the high-value jobs, fewer tire-kickers, and to stop being their own receptionist. They want to look online as good as their work looks in person.

What they've tried: a template they never finished filling in; a relative's WordPress that broke after an update and now shows a white screen; a website-builder free trial still sitting in a browser tab from last spring. The few who asked a city agency for a quote got back a long timeline and an email full of words like "discovery phase," and closed the tab.

Where they are: Instagram is the real shopfront. Local Facebook groups, trade forums, YouTube in the evening, Allegro for supplies. They make buying decisions on a phone, between jobs.

## The Mission
Polish small businesses lose to worse competitors with better websites every single day. A great craftsman with a dead Facebook page gets beaten by an average one with a "book now" button. That isn't fair, and it isn't how it should work.

PROJSTOG exists to flip that. The best local business in town should win the customer — not the one who could afford the biggest agency. If it works, a one-van detailer, a family fabricator, a solo agent gets the same caliber of online presence that used to be reserved for companies with a marketing department, on a timeline that fits how they actually live. The reward goes back to the people doing the best work.

It's replacing the abandoned builder tab, the broken WordPress, the "DM for price" Instagram bio, and the dead Facebook page that counts as a website only because there's nothing else.

## The Unfair Advantage
The moat isn't a feature — it's the shape of the business.

PROJSTOG only builds for Polish local service trades. Every Profit Site feeds a library of conversion-tested layouts, copy, and components built for exactly these businesses — detailers, fabricators, contractors, agents. Each build is faster and converts better than the last, because it inherits what already worked for the same kind of owner in the same kind of town. A generalist agency that says yes to any website for anyone can never accumulate that niche playbook; their next project starts from zero. PROJSTOG's starts from a hundred.

The Live Care relationship does the second half of the work: recurring care keeps PROJSTOG inside the business long after launch, which builds trust, referrals, and a steady stream of conversion data no one-off agency ever sees. Niche data compounds, the ongoing relationship deepens, and a founder who speaks the trade and the language answers in the same time zone. A competitor can copy any one of those. Copying all three means becoming PROJSTOG.

## The Visual Identity
The PROJSTOG design system applies as-is — no redesign. Signal Green (`#1B9D17`) on obsidian, the Voltage accent (`#34E12E`) for live moments, Bone (`#ECE7DD`) for premium weight; Clash Display / Satoshi / Space Mono; the 4px spacing rhythm, 10/16px radii, hairline-and-glow elevation, and the rising-stack "stóg" mark.

Two brand-specific applications:

- **The Profit Teardown report** is delivered as a dark, blueprint-grid document: Space Mono coordinate annotations in the margins, fixes flagged with the semantic Error and Warning tints, and the recommended plan marked in Voltage. The artifact itself looks engineered — proof of the work before the work.
- **The Live Care monthly note** reuses the mobile-app stat treatment from the system: one Bone headline number (quote requests this month), a green bar climbing to a Voltage peak, and a single `Live` status badge. One screen, one number, one signal — the owner sees the site working without reading a report.
